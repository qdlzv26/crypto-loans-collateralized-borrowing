## What Is a Crypto Loan?

A crypto loan is a collateralized borrowing arrangement. Instead of proving your income or undergoing a traditional credit check, you provide eligible digital assets as security for the amount you borrow.

For example, you might deposit BTC as collateral and borrow USDT. You still own the collateral while the loan is active, but the platform can restrict or sell it if your loan becomes too risky or remains unpaid.

The basic relationship is expressed through the loan-to-value ratio, commonly called **LTV**:

text
LTV = Loan value ÷ Collateral value


Suppose you deposit $10,000 worth of BTC and borrow $3,000 in USDT. Your starting LTV is 30%. If BTC falls and your collateral is worth only $7,500, the LTV rises to 40%, even though your borrowed amount has not changed.

That is the central risk of crypto loans. The debt may stay relatively stable while the collateral moves sharply.

Crypto loans are commonly used for:

- Short-term liquidity without selling a long-term crypto position
- Trading or portfolio rebalancing
- Borrowing stablecoins against volatile assets
- Funding another crypto purchase
- Avoiding an immediate taxable sale in situations where local tax rules make that relevant
- Accessing capital while waiting for another payment or settlement

The last point needs caution. Borrowing may delay a sale, but it does not automatically remove tax obligations. Tax treatment depends on your jurisdiction and personal circumstances.

## Why LTV Matters More Than the Advertised Rate

A low interest rate can look attractive while the collateral structure remains dangerous.

Assume you deposit $10,000 of BTC and borrow $3,000. The initial LTV is 30%. If BTC falls by 25%, your collateral is worth $7,500 and your LTV rises to 40%.

You still owe roughly $3,000 plus interest. The loan did not become smaller because the market moved against your collateral.

A conservative borrower should consider three numbers before opening the position:

- **Starting LTV:** How much of the collateral value you are borrowing
- **Liquidation LTV:** The level at which the platform may begin forced repayment or liquidation
- **Available buffer:** How far the collateral can fall before the position becomes unsafe

The platform may also display a health factor or liquidation price. OKX specifically states that Flexible Loan provides LTV monitoring and, for loans with one loan asset and one collateral asset, additional metrics such as health factor and liquidation price.

A loan that is technically below the liquidation threshold may still be uncomfortable if the collateral is highly volatile. Crypto prices can move faster than a borrower can arrange a transfer or add collateral. Borrowing less than the maximum is often the simplest risk-control measure.

## What Happens If the Collateral Falls?

If the loan-to-value ratio reaches the relevant risk threshold, the platform may require additional collateral, reduce the position, or liquidate collateral to repay the liability.

OKX explains that Flexible Loan liquidation or forced repayment can be triggered when the LTV reaches the liquidation LTV or when platform-level risk controls are reached.

For specialized products, the process can be broader. OKX’s Risk Unit documentation states that forced repayment or Credit Line liquidation may freeze accounts within the risk unit, use available funding assets first, and sell assets if liabilities remain.

This is why a crypto loan should not be sized solely around the maximum amount the platform allows. The maximum is a platform limit, not a recommendation.

Before borrowing, decide:

1. How much collateral you are willing to risk.
2. How much of a price drop you can withstand.
3. Whether you can add collateral quickly.
4. Where the repayment funds will come from.
5. Whether you can repay if the borrowed asset appreciates against you.

That last question matters when the loan is denominated in a volatile cryptocurrency. If you borrow BTC and BTC rises, the value of the liability rises in fiat terms as well.

## Repayment and Interest

Flexible Loan is designed for repayment at the borrower’s pace, but flexible repayment does not mean interest-free repayment. Interest continues while the liability remains outstanding, and the rate can change.

Repayment may use the borrowed asset or another supported settlement method, depending on the product and account interface. Confirm the repayment asset before submitting the transaction. Paying back the wrong asset is a surprisingly expensive way to learn how exchange balances work.

VIP Loan follows different rules. The published documentation states that the loan term is 90 days, interest is calculated using the borrowing amount, matched rate, and term, and the full interest is due with the principal at maturity. Early repayment timing can affect the amount charged, while overdue loans may incur additional interest.

For any product, keep records of:

- The borrowed amount
- The borrowing asset
- The collateral deposited
- The rate at the time of borrowing
- The current rate, if floating
- Accrued interest
- The repayment deadline
- The liquidation threshold
- Any transfer or withdrawal restrictions

These details make it easier to judge whether the loan remains economically sensible.

## Is a Crypto Loan Better Than Selling?

There is no universal answer. It depends on why you need liquidity, how volatile the collateral is, and whether you have a credible repayment plan.

Borrowing may make sense when:

- You need temporary liquidity.
- You want to maintain exposure to the collateral asset.
- You have a predictable source of repayment.
- Your starting LTV is conservative.
- You can monitor the position and add collateral if necessary.

Selling may be simpler when:

- You do not have a clear repayment source.
- The collateral is highly volatile.
- Interest is likely to exceed the benefit of holding the asset.
- A liquidation would create a serious financial problem.
- You are borrowing to cover recurring expenses rather than a short-term gap.

A loan preserves exposure to price gains, but it also preserves exposure to price losses while adding interest and liquidation risk. That tradeoff should be visible in the numbers before the loan is opened.

## Common Crypto Loan Mistakes

### Borrowing the maximum amount

The platform’s maximum borrowing limit does not account for your rent, tax obligations, emergency fund, or tolerance for a forced sale. It only describes what the system may permit under current conditions.

### Using volatile collateral for a large loan

A token can be liquid enough to borrow against and still move sharply in price. Starting with a lower LTV creates more room for ordinary market volatility.

### Treating a floating rate as fixed

Flexible Loan rates can change with market conditions. Calculate the loan at several possible interest rates instead of relying only on the rate displayed at the moment you open the position.

### Ignoring the borrowed asset’s price risk

Borrowing BTC is different from borrowing USDT. If the borrowed asset appreciates, the fiat value of the liability can rise.

### Waiting until liquidation is close

Adding collateral during a rapid market decline may be difficult. The safest time to reduce LTV is before the position becomes urgent.

### Assuming every OKX product is available in every region

OKX states that products, features, and promotions may not be available in all jurisdictions. Account eligibility and local restrictions should be checked directly before relying on a product description.
